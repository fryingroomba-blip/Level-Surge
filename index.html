HOW A RUN WORKS

Walk these rooms first.

Scroll through. A shard lights up when your hand is on it. Each one is a rule I'm going to use on you later, so you might as well see it now.

The job

Touch the door with the gold roof and that room is over. Everything else is me getting in the way of that.

  • You die, you come back in the same room. The skull count sticks for the whole world. I don't reset it. Mean.
  • Clear the last room and the next world opens. You have to walk them in order. No skipping, I tried that and it felt cheap.
  • This browser remembers the world, the room, the deaths, and the time. Wipe the site data and I forget you. Sorry. Not really.

Two doors

Same shape on purpose. Look at the roof, that's the tell. I got tired of explaining this to people and then they still picked the dull one.

  • Real one: bright gold roof, red door. Touch it and you leave.
  • Fake one: duller gold. In Knock Knock I put it on the right because that's where everybody runs.
  • Touch the fake and the room can spring. Psych makes you walk all the way back. I still laugh at that one.
  • Boss door stays shut until his moves are used up.

What a trap waits for

A trap waits for something you do, then it answers. Sometimes it answers late, so you think you're fine. You're not fine.

  • Walk past a column. That's most of them. I got lazy and also it works.
  • Jump near the start. Hope is a trigger. I know how that sounds.
  • Don't Stop: stand still for 0.4 seconds and the floor drops. Four tenths. I timed it.
  • Wait For It holds the ceiling, then drops a brick on you at 1.9 seconds.
  • Some of them arm after you pass a mark, then fire when you come back. Psych is the mean version of this.
  • Most of them fire once. A few wake back up after a pause.

What it does to you

Dust and a glow are me whispering. They don't count. The number in the bar waits for something that can actually hit you.

  • A gate, a hole, side teeth, ceiling teeth, a brick. Those take the floor or the air you were using.
  • Saws chase. Lasers cross. A push, a lock, wind, flipped controls, flipped gravity. Those take your hands for a second.
  • The bar stays ???? until a real one fires. A tell leaves it alone. I like the little dread.

The bar

That row across the top is the run. It lies about the trap count until something real fires. I did that on purpose.

  • World, room number, name, little pips. Lit pip is you. Red pips are rooms you already lived through. Wear them.
  • SURGE stacks if you live through real traps inside 2.4 seconds. Die and it's gone. Hit ten and it prints a stupid huge number. I think it's funny.
  • The diamond is shards from coins. Shiny and Last Laugh, those coins blow up. Everywhere else they're safe and I still grab them.
  • Clear a world and the score is 79,437,924 × (90 − deaths) × (260 − seconds). Neither bit goes under 1. I wanted a huge number. Sue me.

Hands

Keyboard and the phone buttons do the same jobs. I was stubborn about dash and people got stuck, so now you know.

  • Move with arrows or A and D. Jump with Space, W, or Up.
  • Dash is Shift. It shows up in Frostbite, world 3. I should have given it to you in world 1. Ledges. My fault.
  • Dash keeps its speed and only fires once per press. Shift plus A or D still walks. I had to fix that twice.
  • R retries the room. Esc pauses. The speaker is the mute.

World 1, by hand

Sixteen rooms, then the boss. I placed every trap myself. If these make sense, the later seeded rooms make more sense. Kind of.

  • Trust Me. Dust, then the floor leaves. I named it that so you'd trust it.
  • Keep Jumping. The landing is already a hole.
  • Knock Knock. Right door is fake. People run right.
  • Wait For It. Ceiling spikes, floor comes back at 1.9 seconds.
  • Shiny. Three coins over spikes. They blow up. I still grab them.
  • Backwards. Left and right swap for a second. I still mess this up.
  • Checkered. Some of the bricks are paint.
  • Buzz. The saw by the door starts following you.
  • Yeet. The pad throws you, then gravity gets light and my stomach drops.
  • Up Is Down. Door's on the ceiling. I almost softlocked myself building it.
  • Come Back. The door slides away while you walk.
  • Fall In. The door is down in the hole. Jump anyway.
  • Don't Stop. 0.4 seconds of standing and the floor drops.
  • Psych. Fake door, then the long walk back.
  • Through. The middle wall is paint. Walk into it and feel stupid with me.
  • Last Laugh. Coins still blow up, right door is fake, real one is waiting when you come back.

How a world grows

Same seed, same pain. Clear one and the next one wakes up. I didn't want it random every time you died.

  • Seeded worlds are 15 rooms plus the world number. World 1 is the weird one: 16 handmade rooms, then boss stage 1. World 20 has 35. Yeah.
  • Floors get 5 blocks wider every world. The later traps needed the room. They were piled on the old 32-wide map and it looked dumb.
  • From world 4, about a quarter of the rooms are shafts. You climb to the door or you drop to it. I kept the steps clean. Traps on stairs felt cheap, so they wait on the flat.
  • Trap count starts at 10. Each world adds the old climb plus 10 more. Boss room doubles that, triples it from world 8, quadruples it from 18. I had a spreadsheet. I lost the spreadsheet. The code remembers.
  • Every world gets two signature traps. They go in first, so you can tell which world bit you.

The cruel stuff

They show up on a schedule. I didn't want the horror guys in world 1. World 1 is already rude.

  • Weird movement tricks start in world 2. One a room, two from world 10. Blackout is world 3 and later, about one room in ten, and it just holds you in the dark for a bit.
  • The horror things start in world 5. Fog needs world 9. The moving safe light needs world 10. I gated them because early on they were just noise.
  • The really cruel traps are rare from world 8 and common from world 10. They get nastier again from world 15. I kept turning the knobs.
  • Button prompts start in world 3, about one room in ten. Miss the key, or wait too long, and you die. The window shrinks as you go up. Presses go from 1 to 6. My hands hate world 19.

The last room

The floor is flat. I got tired of the stairs. The boss is the room, basically.

  • The door sits there until you walk up. After 30 seconds the boss gets bored and drops it somewhere you aren't standing.
  • Null keeps the long fight. His own look, his own music, and he doesn't reuse the same heavy move until it's ready. He throws the door 7 times.
  • From Ashwell on, that count starts at 9 and adds 2 each world. Those bosses fight like their world. Later on they pull out different attacks. A second set, then a last set.
  • A mark shows before a shot so you get a beat. If the shot gets you, that boss talks in their own color. Die to a normal room trap and you still get the old line.
  • When the moves run out the face goes tired, they fall, and they blow up. The blast kills. Door's open after that. Grab it on the way down if you're stubborn.

What you hear

Every dimension has its own unique soundtrack for each dimension, making the game more creative. The about page still plays the old shared mix, the one from before I got obsessed.

  • Boss rooms put their own drums under that world's song. Last Light is slower and heavier. I get a little nervous about it.
  • Moving hazards get louder when they get close. A saw across the room is quiet. A saw on you is not.
  • Tap once if the browser is holding the sound. Speaker in the bar is the mute. Same button, less panic.
